How to read this

Each pattern that broke out upward is bought at that day's close, with the pattern's own stop and target, a 60-bar time limit, T+2 settlement and 0.30% commission each way. The random-day control repeats the same stop and target distances on 20 random days of the same share. The edge is the difference: it is what the pattern adds beyond the stop and target themselves. The line on each bar is a 95% range from resampling whole shares and, separately, whole calendar months (the wider of the two), because one share's trades overlap and many shares break out together when the market moves.

Checks and limits

  • Real-time check. On 40 shares the detector was run on the first 60% of the history and on all of it. Of 1106 trades found on the short history, 1 were absent from the full run; 0 trades in the full run were not found on the short history. A gap means the full run knew something the short run could not (a larger pattern replacing a smaller one), so results are slightly flattering.
  • Skipped. 6867 breakouts happened before the pattern could have been known and were not traded; 141 had a stop or target on the wrong side of the entry; 425 were locked bars.
  • Many patterns, many tests. 9 pattern types were looked at, so one that looks good by luck is expected. Trust a type only if it holds in the liquid subset, in both halves of the history and in the holdout.
  • Not modelled: circuit-breaker limits and the floor-price period of 2020, queue position, slippage beyond the 0.30% (a thin share can cost far more), tick rounding, dividends and taxes. "Liquid" means a median daily turnover of at least BDT 5 million over the 20 days before entry.
  • Trades overlap. The same share can give several overlapping patterns, so the trade count overstates independent evidence. The 95% ranges account for this; the plain counts do not.

Script: scripts/backtest-patterns.mjs. Data: Mendeley A2 adjusted equities to 22 Jan 2026, then the terminal's day-end files adjusted for bonus and rights.
